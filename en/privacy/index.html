Privacy policy

Last updated: October 26, 2026

In short: SquadMeet has no accounts and no ads. Video and audio go directly and encrypted between your browsers: we don’t see or record them. For the free time we count minutes with a random identifier, without knowing who you are. On the website, Google Analytics only runs if you accept it.

Who is responsible

SquadMeet is an independent project. For any privacy question, write to contacto@squadmeet.app.

Your camera and microphone

  • Whoever creates the group: SquadMeet runs inside ome.tv, in their browser. It combines their camera with their friends’ into a single picture and mixes your microphones, and that’s what Ome.tv sends to the stranger. Whatever happens inside Ome.tv is covered by its own privacy policy.
  • Friends: the invitation page asks for the camera and microphone and sends them to whoever created the group, who sends back the stranger’s and the rest of the group’s picture and sound.
  • Everything travels over WebRTC, end-to-end encrypted between browsers (DTLS-SRTP). We don’t record anything and can’t see or hear your conversations. The group chat (Pro) uses the same encrypted channel and doesn’t go through our servers either.

What goes through our servers

  • Signaling: so the browsers can find each other, our server relays some technical messages: random identifiers for the group and each person, the connection description and the network addresses needed (IPs and ports). Friends also send the name they type (optional), their browser language and their phone’s orientation. They’re only kept in memory while the connection lasts: nothing is stored. If our server isn’t available (some carriers sometimes block its IPs), they go through the public PeerJS server, which also just relays them.
  • Relay (TURN): if your networks don’t allow a direct connection, video goes through an ExpressTURN server. It stays end-to-end encrypted; the server sees IPs, not content.
  • Free time: to apply each day’s free minutes, SquadMeet creates a random identifier in your browser and sends our server the minutes you use with friends connected and whether you shared SquadMeet on WhatsApp that day. We keep that identifier, the day and the minutes for 30 days. We don’t link it to your name, your email or your Ome.tv account, and we don’t know who you send the WhatsApp message to.
  • Pro: payment happens on Payhip, the platform we sell Pro through. Payhip and its payment processor (Stripe or PayPal) process your payment details; Payhip gives us your email and the purchase details so we can deliver your key, handle refunds and meet tax obligations, and we keep them for as long as the law requires. When you paste your key, our server asks Payhip whether it’s valid. We keep an encrypted fingerprint of the key (not the key), whether it’s still active and the random identifiers of the devices where you use it (up to 3), while the license is active and 30 more days. That check doesn’t store your email. If you buy from the panel, the panel sends our server the email you’re going to pay with, and Payhip tells us when you pay or get a refund; that’s how we turn Pro on in that browser. We don’t store the email, only an encrypted fingerprint of it, along with what you’ve paid for (and until when, for the 31-day one) and the random identifiers of your devices (up to 3), for up to 400 days after your last payment.
  • The website: squadmeet.app is served by GitHub Pages, which processes visitors’ IPs to deliver the pages and protect them from attacks.

Google Analytics

Only if you press “Accept” in the cookie notice, the website uses Google Analytics 4 to measure how many people visit it, which pages they see, their approximate country and what kind of device they use. It uses cookies (listed in the cookie policy) and the data is kept for 2 months. You can withdraw your permission anytime in “Cookie settings”, at the bottom of the site. Neither the invitation page nor ome.tv has analytics.

What’s stored on your device

  • On ome.tv: the panel’s position, whether you mirror your image, the random free-time identifier, your Pro key if you paste it and, while the tab is open, the group so you can resume it if you reload.
  • On the invitation page: the name you type, so you don’t have to type it again.
  • On this website: where you are in the phone guide and your cookie choice.

You can delete it anytime from your browser’s settings.

Why and on what basis

  • Connecting group members, counting free time and checking Pro licenses: it’s the service you ask for by using it (article 6.1.b GDPR).
  • Preventing abuse and keeping the website and servers secure: legitimate interest (article 6.1.f).
  • Google Analytics: your consent (article 6.1.a), which you can withdraw anytime.

We don’t sell your data, use it for advertising or profiling, or share it with anyone other than the providers below.

Providers and transfers

  • GitHub, Inc. (the website) and Cloudflare, Inc. (signaling, free-time counter and licenses, domain, email and relay credentials). The counter and licenses are stored on servers in the European Union.
  • Payhip Ltd. (Pro sales) and its payment processor (Stripe or PayPal), Google Ireland Ltd. (Analytics, only with permission), PeerJS (backup signaling) and ExpressTURN (relay, only when needed).
  • Some may process data outside the European Economic Area under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and standard contractual clauses.
  • The stores you install the extension from have their own policies.

Your rights

You can request access, rectification, erasure, objection, restriction or portability by writing to contacto@squadmeet.app. Since we don’t know who you are, to find your data we’ll need your Pro key or the random identifier (shown in the SquadMeet panel). If you think we haven’t handled your request properly, you can complain to the Spanish Data Protection Agency (aepd.es) or your local authority.

Minors

SquadMeet is 18+ only, the same age Ome.tv requires.

Changes

If we change this policy, we’ll say so here with a new date.
